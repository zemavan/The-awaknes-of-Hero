Year 2059. Neural networks have gradually taken over humanity's infrastructure. Autonomous systems control communications, energy, transport, and manufacturing. The last independent computing nodes are shutting down one by one.

Vasya was one of the few who understood how the old systems worked. After a car accident, he slipped into a coma. Petya Shnurov, his only close friend, visited him at the hospital every day.

One day, Vasya’s pre-configured communication system activated. Petya received a message from his friend — a short text signal sent straight from the hospital room.

Vasya was alive. And, apparently, he had been trying to finish a certain project all this time.

“Petya. If you are reading this, it means we have one last chance. The server room at the old factory. Find my laptop. Do not trust the network.”

The problem was that Vasya could no longer work on a computer by himself. And Petya barely knew how to use a terminal.

But Vasya could communicate with him through a homemade interface, passing instructions and explaining what needs to be done.
